Tracking bulk orders

Improving efficiency of cement dealers by enhancing the visibility of their placed orders

🔍 Click to enlarge
Disclaimer

While I might not be able to disclose specific details of these projects I had worked on, I will try to demonstrate the thought process and design decisions I took along the way with an hypothetical use case.

CONTEXT

Understanding the importance of dealers in their sale revenue generation, a leading cement manufacturing company based in India decides to launch a dealer's platform specifically tailored to their needs to conduct business smoothly.

During our primary research, we discovered that dealers' inability to track their ordered products' delivery status is a significant barrier to sales. The current offline system relies heavily on phone calls for communication.
This resulted in

Lack of Transparency

Customers are unable to see the real-time status of their orders. This can lead to frustration and uncertainty.

Inefficient Communication

Relying on phone calls can be time-consuming and inefficient. Customers may have to wait on hold or make multiple calls to get information.

To solve for this, I delved deep into the existing business process to find out:

Most of the orders will have multiple line items.
The size of the orders varies depending of the dealers and demand in the market.
The large orders are fulfilled in batches over multiple shipment.

Just like all other ordering apps, the dealers come to Track Order flow mainly to know:

Current status of their orders

2. Getting into the order details

Upon identifying the order, each card takes you the Line Item Cards and their distribution of it over the shipments.

Iteration 1: Expandable Line Item Cards

The initial explorations revolved around limiting the order detail to a single page. The idea here was to progressively disclose the information precisely when he needs it.

🔍 Click to enlarge
Order details - Cards view
🔍 Click to enlarge
Order details - Table view

That didn't work

While these were good directions, it had its own limitations:

1. Maximum shipments bottleneck: It works best if the number of shipments are limited to maximum 6. More than 6 will take push it to third of fourth scroll in very small space. But the average number is higher than six and it can go up to 20.

2. Shipment segregation: The segregation of the shipments (delivered & ongoing) is impossible within a single accordion card.

This actually gave me a real good clarity on the next steps. I chucked the expandable card and split the order detail in to two pages.
